## 1. Project Description

TechNova RAG is an internal retrieval-augmented generation platform that allows TechNova personnel to ask natural-language questions over a fixed corpus of eleven internal PDFs. The system runs a FastAPI backend (`backend/main.py`) fronted by a Next.js 16.2 frontend. Document chunks (500 characters with 100-character overlap, via `langchain-text-splitters 0.3.0`) are embedded with `BAAI/bge-base-en-v1.5` (768-dim) and stored in Qdrant 1.12 (`technova_docs` collection). A BM25 index is persisted at `backend/bm25_index.pkl`. Retrieval hybridises dense and lexical with Reciprocal Rank Fusion (k=60) and re-ranks with `cross-encoder/ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2`. Answers are generated by OpenAI's `gpt-4o-mini`.

The platform has three surfaces: Project A (open demo chat), Project B (role-gated secure chat with self-correcting retrieval), and a 3D knowledge graph. Optionally, chat history and a corpus mirror are written to Neon Postgres via `asyncpg 0.30`.

## 2. Necessity and Proportionality

**Purpose.** Replace slow manual document search across eleven internal policy and operational PDFs with a grounded retrieval experience. Without the platform, personnel spend disproportionate time locating information that has already been authored and classified, and rely on informal forwarding of sensitive content which is itself a higher-risk disclosure path.

**Lawful basis (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)).** Legitimate interests — operational efficiency of the controller's internal knowledge access, balanced against the limited personal data processed and strong access controls described below. A legitimate interests assessment (LIA) is held on file at `industry-documents/legal-and-commercial/LIA.md` (Roadmap v1.1 to publish).

**Proportionality.** The system only indexes PDFs explicitly listed in `DOCUMENT_METADATA` (`backend/config.py`) — the loader skips any other file by design. This bounds the scope of personal data processing to what has been pre-classified.

**Alternatives considered.** (a) Full-text search without generation — rejected because it does not solve the language-of-question mismatch problem. (b) Per-user fine-tuning — rejected because it would require embedding personal data in model weights. (c) External hosted RAG — rejected because RESTRICTED classification requires keeping the corpus on TechNova-controlled infrastructure.

## 3. Data Subject Categories

| Category | Description | Number affected |
|---|---|---|
| Current TechNova employees | Named or implicitly identifiable in HR policy, salary structure, training records, board minutes. | ~[company-size] |
| Former TechNova employees | May persist in archived HR and board documents. | Approx. retention pool |
| Board directors | Named in board minutes. | Single digits |

## 5. Risks to Rights and Freedoms

| Risk | Likelihood | Severity | Inherent rating | Mitigating controls | Residual rating |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R-1 Cross-role disclosure of compensation data to employees below admin clearance | High if unprotected | High | Very High | Dual pre-filter on Qdrant + BM25 (`backend/services/security.py`); Salary_Structure classified RESTRICTED (level 3); access-denied probe | Low |
| R-2 LLM regurgitates restricted data from prior ingestion into grounded answer | Medium | High | High | Grounded prompt in `backend/services/generator.py`; RESTRICTED chunks excluded from the candidate pool, so never reach the prompt | Low |
| R-3 Model hallucinates about an identified employee (accuracy right under Art. 5(1)(d)) | Medium | Medium | Medium | System prompt forbids unsupported claims; answer cites chunks; user advised outputs are advisory and must be verified | Medium (disclosure in user-facing UI) |
| R-4 Profiling of employees via inferred characteristics | Low | High | Medium | System is advisory Q&A only; no automated decision-making; Art. 22 does not apply | Low |
| R-5 Unauthorised external disclosure via prompt injection in PDF | Low | High | Medium | Corpus is TechNova-authored; change control on `/docs/`; `DOCUMENT_METADATA` allowlist | Low |
| R-6 Chat-history log retains sensitive snippets and is exposed via Postgres breach | Low | High | Medium | TLS on asyncpg connection; Neon managed encryption at rest; 90-day retention; Roadmap v1.1 shorter retention for C+R responses | Medium |
| R-7 Sub-processor (OpenAI) incident exposes prompts | Low | High | Medium | Data not used for training by default per API policy; DPA on file; incident notification clause | Medium |
| R-8 Timing side-channel on access-denied emission infers existence of restricted match | Low | Low | Low | Access-denied text is content-free ("Relevant information exists in N documents"); Roadmap v1.2 latency normalisation | Low |
| R-9 Right-to-erasure request for named individual | Medium | Medium | Medium | Re-ingestion flow; chunk-level delete supported via Qdrant filter; BM25 rebuild required | Medium (manual, documented) |

## 7. Measures to Demonstrate Compliance

| GDPR principle | Measure | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Lawfulness, fairness, transparency (Art. 5(1)(a)) | Internal privacy notice + Acceptable Use Policy | `legal-and-commercial/ACCEPTABLE_USE.md` |
| Purpose limitation (Art. 5(1)(b)) | Only indexes `DOCUMENT_METADATA`-listed files; no secondary use of chat history | `backend/config.py`, retention policy |
| Data minimisation (Art. 5(1)(c)) | Only pre-classified PDFs ingested; no crawler | `backend/routers/ingest.py` |
| Accuracy (Art. 5(1)(d)) | Grounded responses; users directed to source docs | `backend/services/generator.py` |
| Storage limitation (Art. 5(1)(e)) | 90-day chat retention; re-ingest on classification change | `backend/services/chat_store.py` |
| Integrity & confidentiality (Art. 5(1)(f)) | TLS, encryption at rest, role-based filter, CORS pinning | `backend/main.py`, `backend/services/security.py` |
| Accountability (Art. 5(2)) | This DPIA; SOC 2 readiness; ISO 27001 SoA; Revision History | Compliance corpus |

Technical measures (specific to the platform):

1. **Dual pre-filter on restricted data** — the core invariant of Project B. Implemented in `get_security_filter` (Qdrant side) and `get_allowed_chunk_ids` (BM25 side).
2. **Grounded prompt assembly** — `backend/services/generator.py` constructs the prompt from top-5 rerank outputs only. The LLM is instructed to answer from provided context.
3. **Access-denied messaging without content leak** — "Relevant information exists in N document(s) that require higher clearance. Contact your department head for access." The count N is a non-sensitive signal accepted by Trust & Security.
4. **Self-correcting loop** — synonym expansion from `_SYNONYMS` dict (salary→{compensation, pay, remuneration}, etc.) widens accessible recall when top-1 rerank is weak, reducing the chance of under-answering.
5. **CORS pinning** — `http://localhost:3000` and regex `https://*.vercel.app`.
6. **Secret management** — `.env` gitignored; secrets injected via Vercel + deployment secret manager.
7. **Sub-processor contractual controls** — DPAs with OpenAI, Neon, Qdrant Cloud, Vercel; see `SUB_PROCESSORS.md`.

## 8. Residual Risk and Justification

After mitigations, the overall residual risk is assessed **Medium-Low**. The largest residual concerns are:

- R-3 (model hallucination about a named employee) — medium. Accepted with user education and roadmap improvements (citation hover preview, cross-document confidence score).
- R-6 (chat-history breach) — medium. Neon's managed encryption and short retention reduce but do not eliminate the risk.
- R-7 (sub-processor incident at OpenAI) — medium. Addressed contractually; out-of-scope to fully eliminate without moving to a self-hosted LLM (evaluation on roadmap).

The system does not engage in Art. 22 automated decision-making. There is no profiling sufficient to produce legal or similarly significant effects on data subjects. The balancing test under Art. 6(1)(f) weighs in favour of processing.

## 9. DPO Sign-Off

I have reviewed this DPIA and the underlying technical measures. In my opinion, the processing described proceeds with adequate safeguards and does not require prior consultation of the supervisory authority under Art. 36.
